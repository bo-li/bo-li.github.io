Direction 1 · Coordination chemistry

Rare-earth & f-element separation

Metal separation depends on coupled changes in coordination, solvation, and solution composition. My work connects element-specific selectivity to coordination preferences rather than ionic size alone, and organizes extraction data so chemical trends can be told apart from differences in measurement conditions.

Direction 2 · Reaction mechanisms & generative AI

CO₂ capture & conversion

Predicting capture from the stability of a single adduct can miss reactions that change which molecular states are accessible. My mechanistic studies show how proton transfer and rearrangement open pathways beyond the first CO₂ addition, and I use those insights to steer generative AI toward new capture chemistry.

J. Chem. Inf. Model. 2026 Current work

Property-guided generative AI discovers CO₂-reactive carbanions

A nucleophilicity model trained on Mayr’s reactivity database steers a hierarchical variational autoencoder toward reactive carbanions. DFT validation shows where the predictions hold: cyano- and ester-stabilized carbanions.

Direction 3 · Structure, dynamics, transport

Ionic systems: molten salts to solid electrolytes

When long-range order is absent, transport is set by local coordination, network connectivity, and collective dynamics. I have followed this question from molten chlorides for nuclear energy to glassy solid electrolytes for batteries.

U–Cl–U
J. Mol. Liq. 2020

Chloride-bridged uranium networks in molten NaCl–UCl₃

Polarizable-ion and first-principles simulations show how rising UCl₃ content builds Cl-bridged networks of uranium polyhedra with coupled U³⁺/Cl⁻ motion, and how U(III) and U(IV) differ.

Paper → UCl₃ vs UCl₄ (2019) → Adding MgCl₂ (2022) →
Eu²⁺/Eu³⁺
Inorg. Chem. 2023

Solvent cations reshape europium coordination in molten chlorides

From KCl to CaCl₂, chloride coordination around Eu rises, as confirmed by EXAFS; a more rigid first shell means slower diffusion. At Los Alamos I also built DFTB parameters for f-block chlorides, combined with machine learning, for larger simulations.
