I am a Research Assistant Professor in Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering at Vanderbilt University.

I use quantum chemistry, statistical mechanics, molecular simulation, and machine learning to understand how molecular structure sets the performance of rare-earth and f-element separations, reactive CO₂ capture, and ion-conducting materials.

Previously, I modeled molten salts and f-element chemistry for nuclear energy at Los Alamos National Laboratory, and uranium extraction from seawater at the University of California, Riverside. I hold a PhD in Theoretical Chemistry from the Technical University of Munich.

I am working towards models that explain experiments and know their own limits, so that separation agents, capture molecules, and electrolytes can be designed rather than found by trial and error.

Rare-earth & f-element separation

Selectivity in metal separation comes from coordination, solvation, and solution conditions acting together, not from ionic size alone.

REEL-QUEST: A Condition-Resolved Rare-Earth Extraction Knowledge Base

Rare-earth extraction data mix ligand chemistry with acidity, anion concentration, diluent, and preparation protocol. REEL-QUEST, which I lead, links 4,326 condition-resolved log D measurements across 177 ligands to their reported conditions and source evidence. It keeps measured, calculated, and inferred quantities distinct, so that chemical trends can be told apart from differences in measurement conditions.

A Chameleon-Like Ligand That Recognizes Specific Lanthanides

Removing one preorganization element from an N,O-tetradentate ligand shifts its peak selectivity from light toward middle lanthanides over time, and at high nitric acid concentration heavy lanthanides are extracted as anionic nitrate complexes. I contributed to explaining how coordination and solvation, rather than ionic radius alone, set this selectivity.

J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2024

How Amidoxime Ligands Extract Uranium from Seawater

Free-energy simulations map how amidoxime-based ligands displace carbonate from Ca₂UO₂(CO₃)₃, the dominant uranyl species in seawater. Calcium ions ease removal of the first two carbonates, the third is markedly uphill, and chelating glutardiamidoximes are thermodynamically favored. Related work showed how common salt ions change uranyl–carbonate binding.

Dalton Trans. 2018·J. Phys. Chem. B 2017

Solvation Models for Actinide Chemistry

During my PhD we implemented a three-dimensional reference interaction site model for bulk water around uranyl complexes, with the first solvation shell treated quantum mechanically. The resulting water-exchange barrier agrees well with experiment.

J. Phys. Chem. A 2015

CO₂ capture & conversion

Capacity and reversibility depend on the whole network of accessible reaction states, not just the first CO₂ adduct, and those mechanisms can steer generative design.

Discovering CO₂-Reactive Carbanions via Property-Guided Generative AI

A nucleophilicity model trained on Mayr’s reactivity database guides a hierarchical variational autoencoder toward carbanions that react readily with CO₂. The workflow generated 42,513 novel candidates with 98% chemical validity, and DFT validation defines where its predictions can be trusted: carbanions stabilized by strong electron-withdrawing groups such as cyano and ester.

J. Chem. Inf. Model. 2026

Intermolecular Proton Transfer Enables CO₂ Capture by the Malononitrile Anion

Malononitrile-based ionic liquids capture CO₂ as a carboxylic acid, but how the proton moves was unclear. DFT shows the intramolecular route is blocked by a 152 kJ/mol barrier, while proton transfer between two carboxylate adducts needs only 50 kJ/mol, and the resulting acid dimer is stabilized by hydrogen bonding.

J. Phys. Chem. B 2024

Cascade CO₂ Insertion in Carbanion Ionic Liquids

Carbanions with a conjugated, asymmetric structure take up two CO₂ molecules through consecutive C–C and O–C bond formation. Proton transfer and structural rearrangement after the first insertion create the second binding site and lower the energy needed for release. I contributed to the computational explanation of the pathway.

J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2024

From Capture to Conversion: Arylated Gold Nanoclusters

With experimental collaborators, electronic-structure analysis relates the structure of arylated gold nanoclusters to their CO₂-reduction performance, connecting the local chemical environment to a conversion reaction.

Chem. Sci. 2025

Ionic systems: molten salts to solid electrolytes

Without long-range order, transport is governed by local coordination, network connectivity, and collective dynamics, from molten chlorides for nuclear energy to glassy electrolytes for batteries.

Chloride-Bridged Uranium Networks in Molten NaCl–UCl₃

Polarizable-ion simulations show how increasing UCl₃ content changes uranium coordination and builds chloride-bridged networks of uranium polyhedra with coupled U³⁺ and Cl⁻ motion. First-principles studies compared U(III) with U(IV) and followed how added MgCl₂ disrupts these networks.

J. Mol. Liq. 2020·ACS Appl. Energy Mater. 2019·ChemPhysChem 2022

Solvent Cations Reshape Europium Coordination in Molten Chlorides

First-principles simulations, validated by EXAFS, show chloride coordination around Eu³⁺ rising from 5.9 in KCl to 7.0 in CaCl₂ as the outer-sphere cation becomes more polarizing; a more rigid first shell means slower diffusion. At Los Alamos I also developed density-functional tight-binding parameters for f-block chlorides, combined with machine learning, to reach larger simulations.

Inorg. Chem. 2023

Dynamic “Monkey-Bar” Superionic Li⁺ Transport in Glassy LiTaCl₆

Machine-learning force-field simulations reproduce the measured activation energy for lithium diffusion (0.164 eV vs. 0.165 eV) and reveal the mechanism: large-amplitude chloride vibrations let Li⁺ hop by breaking and re-forming Li–Cl bonds between TaCl₆⁻ octahedra. Follow-up work predicts 15.7 mS cm⁻¹ at room temperature for LiNb₀.₅Ta₀.₅Cl₆.

Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2024·Adv. Funct. Mater. 2024
